# Shared research library

The library preserves source evidence while helping a researcher browse, read, cite,
and record meaningful research use.

## Language

**Acquisition**: Obtaining a particular local copy and preserving where it came
from, which files belong to it, and what the source claimed about it. Having
the files does not establish that they are readable, fully indexed, or verified.

**Intake**: Preparing an explicitly selected acquisition or existing book for
reading, searching, and optional evidence-backed review. Its stages can finish at
different times; useful text need not wait for every metadata question.

**Intake authorization**: Permission for particular preparation stages on the
selected source copy. A future-import preference does not retroactively grant
permission to an earlier intake.

**Local-only intake**: Preparation that omits external semantic processing and
AI metadata review for that intake. It does not prohibit a later separately
authorized operation from processing the same book.

**Intake context**: Source information and inspection findings supplied with a
book, including identifiers, file order, evidence, and discrepancies. A claim
from a provider remains distinguishable from a fact observed in the actual copy.

**Intake coverage**: The account of which book details and indexed headings
were considered, how much source text was inspected, and what remains
unresolved. Unrequested review is not completed review, and a processed heading
is not necessarily fully inspected or verified.

**Outline node**: A source-supported heading or grouping within a book. It may
organize other nodes, contain readable text, or do both.

**Reading unit**: A complete chapter, contribution, or other coherent section
opened for reading, including its supported internal headings and source notes.

**Contribution**: An individual essay, poem, or similar work within a larger
book. Establishing its boundary does not, by itself, verify its authorship.

**Scripture reading**: A biblical text being discussed, such as Jeremiah
15:15–21, distinct from the commentary written about it.
_Avoid_: Passage when the distinction from an indexed passage matters.

**Indexed passage**: A bounded chunk used to retrieve source text. It need not
correspond to a chapter, contribution, or other meaningful source division.
_Avoid_: Chapter, contribution, reading unit.

**Selection**: An exact range of source text chosen for citation or recording
research use. Its wording and source location remain distinct from the scope
and provenance of its bibliographic credit.

**Structural review**: A check of supported boundaries and relationships in a
book. Readable text, complete semantic coverage, and verified bibliography do
not independently establish structural completeness.

**Correction scope**: The book, section, or indexed passage whose information
an edit changes. A reading unit or quotation can have a different extent from
the information that supplies its citation.
Book details, including ISBN relationships, publication dates, publishers, and
editions, belong to the book review. A section review concerns that section and
links to its book details when needed.

**Review recommendation**: An explained course of action for a review question.
It can propose related changes or recommend keeping the current information.
Choosing a recommendation queues any changes; a final save applies them.

**Book format**: The form in which a publication is supplied, such as hardback,
paperback, EPUB, or PDF. Different formats can have different ISBNs without
different contents. An accessible export describes the copy being read and need
not be the same product as a commercial e-book listing.

**Edition**: A published version of a work, sometimes identified by an explicit
edition statement or revision. A change in format or download date alone does
not establish a new edition or a change in text.

**AI support**: An explanation of how strongly the available evidence supports
an AI recommendation, including what remains uncertain. It is distinct from a
person's verification and does not by itself make a citation ready.

**Proposed correction**: An unsaved alternative to recorded information,
accompanied by a reason or source evidence. A proposal does not change what the
library uses, even when its evidence appears convincing.
_Avoid_: Provisional annotation, saved correction.

**Accepted correction**: A proposed correction selected for a final save. It
remains unsaved and does not yet change the library's recorded information.
_Avoid_: Saved correction, verified correction.

**Saved correction**: A change explicitly saved into the library's recorded
information. It can remain provisional while its evidence awaits verification.

**Automatic correction**: A correction saved by authorized intake after its
evidence passes the automatic eligibility rules. It is provisional assistant
work, not a person's acceptance or verification. It can be undone without
overwriting later edits.

**Review decision**: A person's choice about a suggested correction or an
unanswered question. Accepting, rejecting, keeping current information, or
deferring an item does not by itself verify metadata. Keeping current
information closes the question without asserting that the current value is
correct or that an unknown value is absent.

**Deferred question**: An unanswered question set aside for later. It remains
outstanding and can be resumed without starting a new AI review.

**Investigation lead**: A source location or external record that may help
answer a review question. A lead is not supporting evidence until its relevance,
edition, and scope have been checked.

**Verification**: A person's explicit confirmation that identified evidence
supports a particular value, role, edition, and correction scope. Accepting a
suggestion does not by itself verify it or other fields.
